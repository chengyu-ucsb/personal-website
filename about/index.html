About

About me.

I study the everyday communication through which people make decisions, work together, and make sense of new technologies.

I am a Visiting Assistant Professor at the University of Cincinnati. I study how people work together, negotiate expertise, and make sense of new technologies. Organizational communication gives me a way to look closely at decisions that can seem routine until something changes.

Some of my research starts with a simple question: how does a group decide what to do when its usual approach no longer works? I have studied legal professionals evaluating AI, researchers collaborating across disciplines, and teams whose work unfolds in public. In each setting, I pay attention to the conversations, documents, and small adjustments through which people get the work done.

My projects use different methods, including observation, surveys, experiments, network analysis, and computational text analysis. I like following an idea from a concrete interaction to a broader pattern, then checking whether that pattern makes sense in the setting where it began.

Current projects look at AI and knowledge production, AI and education, and AI and resilience. These are still works in progress. I am interested in how people assess an AI-generated answer, decide who should check it, and work out when a tool helps them do their jobs.

I teach organizational communication, research methods, and theory. I want students to see how an idea helps explain a workplace they know, and to notice where the evidence calls for a better explanation. Assignments often ask them to work through a real case, talk with people doing the work, or build a question they can investigate themselves.

I am still getting to know Cincinnati. Outside work, I enjoy exploring a city on foot and catching up with people over coffee.
